- **Title:** Laminar: A Scalable Asynchronous RL Post-Training Framework
- **One-Sentence Summary:** Laminar replaces globally synchronized rollout batches with trajectory-level asynchrony: each trajectory is generated and consumed independently, relay workers provide asynchronous weight synchronization, and dynamic repack consolidates long-tail trajectories to reduce rollout bubbles and staleness.
- **Paper Type:** RL / rollout infrastructure system
- **Venue:** arXiv v1, 2025-10-14; accepted at EuroSys 2026
- **Authors:** Guangming Sheng, Yuxuan Tong, Borui Wan, Wang Zhang, Chaobo Jia, Xibin Wu, Yuqi Wu, Xiang Li, Chi Zhang, Yanghua Peng, Haibin Lin, Xin Liu, Chuan Wu
- **Affiliations:** The University of Hong Kong; ByteDance Seed
- **Resources:** [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.12633); [EuroSys 2026 accepted papers](https://2026.eurosys.org/papers.html); local `paper.pdf` / `paper.md`
- **Keywords:** asynchronous RL, trajectory-level asynchrony, rollout long tail, global weight synchronization, relay worker, dynamic repack, KVCache utilization, inherent staleness, fault tolerance

- ## Orientation
    - **Background:** RL post-training increasingly spends most of its time in rollout generation. Reasoning, coding, tool-calling, and agentic tasks produce highly skewed trajectory lengths and external-environment latencies.
      evidence:: E1, E2
    - **Core Problem:** Synchronous systems wait for a full rollout batch. Existing asynchronous systems often still synchronize all rollouts with a fixed `k-step staleness` schedule. Both designs are bottlenecked by long-tail trajectories or by a hard-to-tune staleness tradeoff.
      evidence:: E2, E3
    - **Key Idea:** Treat each trajectory as the unit of asynchrony. A trajectory finishes at its own pace, enters an experience buffer, and is sampled by the trainer independently. Rollouts update weights when they finish or are released.
      evidence:: E4, E5
    - **Why It Matters Here:** Laminar is not streaming-context RL, but it is strong evidence that rollout long tails, staleness, and global synchronization are systems-level bottlenecks in RL infrastructure.
      claim_kind:: analyst_assessment

- ## Quick Reference
    - **Main Contribution:** Laminar argues that scalable async RL needs trajectory-level asynchrony, not only generation-training overlap.
      evidence:: E4, E5
    - **Rollout Unit:** A complete trajectory generated by one fixed policy version. It can be a single-turn math response or a multi-turn tool-calling/code-sandbox interaction.
      evidence:: E2, E3, E8
    - **Trainer Input:** Completed trajectories from the experience buffer. Experiments use GRPO-style grouped rollouts with rule-based reward, a global batch size of 8192, and 512 prompts with 16 responses each.
      evidence:: E5, E8
    - **Architecture:** Fully decoupled/disaggregated. Trainer GPUs and rollout GPUs are separated; actor/critic/reference can be colocated on trainer GPUs; rollout manager, data pools, experience buffer, and relay workers are separate components.
      evidence:: E5, E8
    - **Weight Synchronization:** The actor pushes weights to a master CPU relay and immediately resumes training. The master relay broadcasts to per-machine relays via RDMA. A rollout pulls weights locally from its colocated relay over PCIe when needed.
      evidence:: E6
    - **Long-Tail Handling:** Dynamic repack monitors KVCache utilization and consolidates unfinished long-tail trajectories, within the same policy-version group, onto fewer destination rollouts. Released rollouts fetch newer weights and continue generation.
      evidence:: E7
    - **Staleness Model:** Laminar avoids a configured staleness bound. It defines inherent staleness as `M-K`, where a trajectory generated with version `K` completes when the actor has reached version `M`.
      evidence:: E8, E9

- ## Argument Map
    - **Problem:** Generation can dominate RL iteration time, reaching 83.1% in the paper's cited reasoning workload. Trajectory length and environment latency are highly skewed.
      evidence:: E1, E2
    - **Prior Limitation 1:** Synchronous and colocated systems wait for complete batches, so short trajectories cannot be consumed early.
      evidence:: E2, E3
    - **Prior Limitation 2:** One-step/k-step async systems overlap generation and training, but still depend on global weight synchronization. Small `k` leaves bubbles; large `k` harms convergence.
      evidence:: E3
    - **Prior Limitation 3:** Partial rollout interrupts ongoing generation, updates weights, and resumes. This can cause repeated KVCache re-prefill and mixed-policy trajectories.
      evidence:: E3, E12
    - **Laminar Claim:** Remove global synchronization, keep each trajectory generated by a single policy version, and let staleness emerge from actual trajectory latency rather than a fixed bound.
      evidence:: E4, E8

- ## Mechanism and Design
    - **Fully Decoupled Architecture:** Data dependency is decoupled because the trainer samples from an experience buffer. Parameter dependency is decoupled because rollouts pull weights through relay workers instead of joining a global sync.
      evidence:: E5
    - **Data Module:** The prompt pool supplies initial tasks, the partial response pool stores in-progress trajectories for recovery, and the experience buffer stores completed trajectories for sampling.
      evidence:: E5
    - **Relay Workers:** Each rollout machine hosts a CPU relay. A master relay receives new actor weights, reshards/broadcasts them through RDMA, and allows local rollout pulls without blocking GPU computation.
      evidence:: E6
    - **Dynamic Repack:** The rollout manager periodically detects rollouts in long-tail generation, groups them by policy version, and moves unfinished trajectories from source rollouts to destination rollouts.
      evidence:: E7
    - **Idleness Metric:** KVCache utilization is used as a workload-independent signal. Once waiting trajectories are exhausted, KVCache usage drops from its peak; the rollout becomes a repack candidate.
      evidence:: E7
    - **Packing Algorithm:** The repack planner uses a Best-Fit style heuristic, bounded by KVCache capacity and roofline batch size, to maximize released source rollouts with small overhead.
      evidence:: E7
    - **Fault Tolerance:** In-progress trajectories are retained in the partial response pool. Rollout machines can be restarted or replaced; relay broadcast chains can be rebuilt around failures.
      evidence:: E5, E6, E11

- ## Evaluation and Evidence
    - **Setup:** 128 machines, 1024 H800-80GB GPUs, Qwen2.5 7B/32B/72B models, DAPO-Math-17k, math reasoning and multi-turn tool-calling, GRPO with rule-based reward.
      evidence:: E9
    - **Throughput:** On math tasks, Laminar reports average speedups of 2.56x over verl, 1.98x over one-step staleness, 1.93x over stream generation, and 1.39x over AReaL, with peak speedup around 5.48/5.49x. Tool-calling shows 2.62x average speedup across baselines.
      evidence:: E10
    - **Scalability:** At the largest cluster sizes, Laminar averages 3.34x speedup across baselines and model sizes.
      evidence:: E10, E13
    - **Convergence:** Wall-clock convergence is 1.77x faster for 7B and 1.59x faster for 32B than the best baseline in the math task.
      evidence:: E10
    - **Weight Sync:** Relay workers reduce rollout waiting time by up to 37% on average and 47% in the best case; actor stall is 0.64s for 32B and 1.40s for 72B.
      evidence:: E11
    - **Repack:** Dynamic repack improves generation throughput by 26%, raises average KVCache utilization from 71.6% to 82.2%, and adds only 0.69s overhead.
      evidence:: E11
    - **Failure Recovery:** After killing a rollout machine with two replicas, the system recovers in about 252s without a global restart.
      evidence:: E11

- ## Technical Judgment
    - **What Holds Up:** Laminar clearly explains why adding rollout GPUs does not solve long-tail rollout if the system still has global batch or global weight-sync barriers.
      claim_kind:: analyst_assessment
    - **Most Useful Lesson:** Staleness is modeled as a property of each completed trajectory, not a single fixed training hyperparameter. This is useful for any RL control plane that tracks freshness.
      claim_kind:: analyst_assessment
    - **Partial Rollout Critique:** The paper's strongest critique of partial rollout is practical and semantic: KVCache re-prefill wastes compute, and a trajectory generated by multiple policy versions is harder to train on cleanly.
      claim_kind:: analyst_assessment
    - **Boundary With AReaL / Relax:** AReaL emphasizes interruption/partial rollout; Relax emphasizes async decoupling and micro-batch streaming for omni-modal RL; Laminar emphasizes removing global synchronization while preserving one policy version per trajectory.
      claim_kind:: analyst_assessment
    - **Limits:** The paper evaluates math and tool-calling, not streaming video context. Experience sampling is left for future work, and no official public code entry is provided in the arXiv/EuroSys pages checked.
      claim_kind:: analyst_assessment
    - **Project Takeaway:** Laminar is not a streaming RL method, but it should be treated as a must-read async rollout systems baseline for any claim about rollout long tails, staleness, and version consistency.
      claim_kind:: analyst_assessment

- ## Workflow Extraction
    - **Initial models:** Qwen2.5 7B/32B/72B; convergence uses Qwen2.5-Math-7B and Qwen2.5-32B.
    - **Initial data:** DAPO-Math-17k for math reasoning and multi-turn tool-calling.
    - **Rollout unit:** Complete trajectory, generated with one consistent policy version.
    - **Rollout input:** Prompts from a prompt pool; tool-calling tasks interact with a code sandbox.
    - **Rollout output:** Completed trajectories plus metadata; in-progress state is streamed to a partial response pool.
    - **Trainer input:** Completed trajectories from the experience buffer; grouped by prompt for GRPO-style updates.
    - **Reward:** Rule-based reward for math and tool-calling.
    - **Trainer update:** GRPO with Clip-Higher in experiments; actor pushes new weights to the master relay after updates.
    - **Resource mode:** Trainer and rollout GPUs are separated; CPU relays are colocated with rollout machines; rollout manager handles monitoring, repack, and recovery.
    - **Streaming-RL implication:** If streaming RL requires context/version consistency inside one sample, Laminar's complete-trajectory single-version design is easier to audit than partial rollout, but streaming sample sealing still needs a separate definition.
