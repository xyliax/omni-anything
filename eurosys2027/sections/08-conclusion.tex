\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\systemname uses cyclic KV restoration to share GPU capacity across periodic interaction sessions while preserving their selected histories.
Its residency plan couples phase assignment, partial eviction, and restoration timing so that space released by idle state can prepare upcoming updates.
The central constraint is both temporal and spatial: a restoration needs enough transfer time and a complete GPU destination before copying begins.
Planning sizes session state at the configured maximum context and reuses the schedule as histories grow within that limit.
In single-run H100 measurements with MiniCPM-o 4.5, long initial histories, and a 48\,GiB GPU KV budget, the evaluated configuration serves 14 sessions versus 11 under full residency.
It completes all 168 admitted updates within their planned two-second deadlines, whereas matched on-demand restoration misses three.
In the common post-startup window, advance restoration reduces periodic p95 completion latency by 58.5\%, with a 22.6\% reduction when measured from source readiness.
These finite-window observations demonstrate a useful capacity--latency tradeoff; cross-model resource estimates remain analytical and do not extend the measured gains to untested workloads.
