\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Applications without phase control.}
An application may expose future ticks while requiring fixed release phases.
Advance restoration can still use that timing information, but the manager cannot redistribute demand by assigning phases.
The benefit then depends on the supplied schedule and available memory.
The phase ablation in Section~\ref{sec:eval-ablation} separates these two sources of benefit.

\paragraph{Finite-horizon admission.}
A finite prediction horizon limits how far admission can anticipate history growth and interference.
Smooth growth can permit infrequent revisions, but it does not guarantee that a feasible schedule exists indefinitely.
If state keeps growing, memory, host capacity, or recovery time can eventually become limiting.
The reported capacity is consequently tied to session duration, the prediction horizon, and the policy for handling infeasible revisions.

\paragraph{Using spare computation for recovery.}
Recomputation could extend cyclic restoration when H2D bandwidth becomes limiting.
After using a selected KV range, the manager could release it and rebuild it before the next tick from retained historical inputs.
This would be a recurring recovery choice, not only a fallback after a failed transfer.
Its compute budget must come from slack across the admitted workload; the idle interval of one session is insufficient if other sessions occupy the GPU.

Combining loading with recomputation is established in prior work.
Cake recomputes earlier prefix chunks while loading later chunks, exploiting the lower attention cost of earlier positions~\cite{jin2025cake}.
For multimodal sessions, applying such a division also requires the appropriate replay inputs, positions, dependencies, and temporary memory.
A shorter period reduces the time available for both transfers and recomputation, so it does not by itself favor recomputation.

\paragraph{Workload and platform dependence.}
A small retained history leaves little memory to reclaim, and a short interval between uses leaves little time to restore it.
Effective transfer bandwidth, host-copy availability, and batching efficiency determine the useful eviction budget.
Different periods or execution profiles can also produce unequal demands despite uniformly spaced phases.
Uniform spacing spreads release times but does not optimize for these differences in resource demand.
